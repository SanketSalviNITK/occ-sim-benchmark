# External anchor feasibility (from abstracts / open-access text only; nothing implemented)

| Paper | Access | BER/success vs distance? | Setup detail enough to anchor our LED-matrix simulator? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lain, Yang, Xu, "Experimental DCO-OFDM OCC Systems With a Commercial Smartphone Camera", IEEE Photonics J. 11(6), 2019, doi:10.1109/JPHOT.2019.2948071 | Open access (CC-BY 4.0, per Crossref/DOAJ) | Yes: Fig. 12, BER vs LED–phone distance at R = 13.8 kbps (P-CVS, D-ELFC, OOK-MEA); best BER 1.763e-3 at 20 cm; BER below 20 % FEC limit when illuminance > 220 lux; >22 kbps at 20 cm (~510 lux) | Single LED, rolling-shutter DCO-OFDM, smartphone camera; distances in the cm range; reported in illuminance. | Narasimman, Alphones, "Simulation and Experimental Validation of OCC", TENCON 2024, pp.1135–1138, doi:10.1109/TENCON61640.2024.10902824 (arXiv:2504.10224) | arXiv preprint open | Yes in substance: detection success rate, simulated vs experimental, for two cameras, two exposure times, a range of switching frequencies and distances | Abstract/conclusion only checked; single-light rolling-shutter on/off detection; detector is "a simple detection technique". | Closest in spirit (sim-vs-experiment on success rate); would need the full arXiv text for camera/LED parameters. | Moreno, Rabadan, Guerra, Perez-Jimenez, "An End-to-End Sensor-Aware OCC Simulator with Application to Intra-Satellite Links", Electronics 15(17) 3906, 2026, doi:10.3390/electronics15173906 | Open access (CC-BY 4.0) | No measured BER vs distance: experiment shows no bit errors (finite-length upper bounds); simulated BER is theoretical from image-domain SNR. Conclusion: none of the three provides an LED-matrix BER-vs-distance curve with enough setup detail for a quantitative anchor;
hardware validation (M4) remains open.
